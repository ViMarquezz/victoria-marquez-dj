About Victoria

Still finding
her sound.

Victoria Veylor didn't plan to become a DJ. She went to her first EDM show and never recovered. The crowd, the energy, the way a single drop could make a thousand strangers feel the exact same thing at the exact same time — that was it. She was in.

She's still finding her sound. And honestly? That's the point. Victoria plays everything — country, cumbia, corridos, reggaeton, classic rock, EDM — and she's not afraid to blend things that have no business being together just to see what happens. Sometimes it shouldn't work. Sometimes it's the best thing in the room.

What she's chasing isn't a genre. It's a moment. The one where the floor fills up, the vibe locks in, and everyone in the room feels it together. Cosmic, collective, unrepeatable.

The approach

The art
of the set.

Music is how a room breathes. Victoria treats every set as a living arc — building, releasing, building again.

Read the room, not the playlist

No two crowds breathe the same way. Victoria watches who's on the floor, what's making them move, what's holding them back — then stays a song ahead of where the energy wants to go next.

Chasing the cosmic moment

Every set is a hunt for the moment no playlist could have planned — when the room locks in and everyone in it feels the same thing at the same time. That's what she's after. Always.

Blends that shouldn't work

Cumbia into EDM. Corridos into hip hop. Country into reggaeton. The transition that makes someone stop and ask "wait — what is this?" The best moments come from genres that have no business meeting. Victoria looks for them on purpose.

What you book is what you get

No bait-and-switch. The setup, the energy, the hours — what's quoted is what shows up. Honest communication from the first message to the last song.

Offline. Always.

Every track is downloaded, organized, and ready in Serato before the first guest arrives. Backyards, garages, intimate venues — your music never depends on WiFi or a stream that might drop.

Your do-not-play list is law

If you said no, it stays off the deck. No exceptions. No "just one time." A visible do-not-play crate is maintained all night so the music respects your boundaries as much as the moment.
